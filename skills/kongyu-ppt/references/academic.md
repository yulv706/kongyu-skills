# Academic Presentation Mode

Load this for conference talks, group meetings, thesis defenses, paper presentations, grant briefings, and other settings where the audience evaluates reasoning and evidence.

Academic mode strengthens evidence discipline; it does not impose a generic white-and-blue template, a fixed page roster, or a paper-like layout.

## Argument and evidence

- Choose the one claim the available time can defend. The full paper is source material, not the slide outline.
- State the research or design question early enough that the audience can evaluate later evidence. It need not occupy a standalone slide when the opening already makes it explicit.
- Distinguish source fact, observed result, interpretation, design choice, hypothesis, and open question.
- For each important claim, record the source figure/table/page/code path or the reasoning that supports it.
- Show uncertainty, limitations, negative results, and alternative explanations before the conclusion becomes stronger than the evidence.
- Rebuild or re-annotate paper figures for projection when their original labels and captions are too small. Do not silently redraw values.

## Slide decisions

- Choose title form from its role and the user's preference. For research maps and explanations, prefer specific noun phrases naming the topic, relation or process; use assertion titles when stating the result is the point of the page. Do not require every title to be a claim, action or question, and avoid generic “Background/Method/Results” labels that hide the actual subject.
- A result page may show more than one exhibit only when the exhibits form one comparison and share one conclusion.
- Put the interpretation next to the mark, state, equation, or table cell it explains. Do not make the audience shuttle between distant bullets and a figure.
- Methods content earns main-deck space only when the audience needs it to trust or interpret the result. Put procedural completeness in the appendix or reading document.
- Keep citations on the slide when they are needed to identify borrowed evidence or constrain a claim. Do not fill every page with a bibliography when a compact source line is enough.
- End the main story on the defensible conclusion and its boundary. “Thank you” may appear unobtrusively, but it should not replace the conclusion left on screen for discussion.

## Academic QA

- Can a domain reader trace each central claim to evidence?
- Does the title chain reproduce the research argument rather than the paper's section headings?
- Does each figure expose the intended finding at projection size?
- Are correlation/causation, implemented/proposed, measured/inferred, and known/unknown boundaries explicit?
- Are likely objections answered in the main story when they threaten the conclusion, with lower-priority robustness material prepared for discussion?
- Does the spoken run fit the actual slot, including explanation and questions? Use rehearsal evidence; do not infer timing from a fixed slides-per-minute formula.

## What was intentionally not carried forward

Do not universalize these common academic-deck recipes: one mandatory research-question slide, one exhibit per page in all cases, figures always left and bullets always right, a fixed 40-word cap, Arial/Calibri-only typography, a compulsory contact/QR slide, or one slide per minute. They are heuristics whose value depends on venue, audience, evidence, and visual system.

## Boundary

This mode owns academic evidence and argument constraints. It does not own the general story method (see [directing.md](directing.md)), visual style (`kongyu-surface`), or PPTX implementation (`ppt-master`).
